# Important Deadlines

The primary concern of the U.S. government is that cloud service providers with an active FedRAMP Certification migrate to the vulnerability management and reporting approach outlined in the FedRAMP VDR/VER rules swiftly, safely, and appropriately. We want to assure all FedRAMP Certified cloud services that FedRAMP will not take unnecessary administrative action against any cloud service, and that any cloud service working safely and in good faith to migrate to the VDR/VER rules will receive support from FedRAMP with minimal adverse administrative action.

This Notice and related material uses the following conventions:

- Administrative action: An official enforcement action taken by FedRAMP to enforce FedRAMP Certification requirements. Administrative action ranges from a notification that corrective action is required, to requesting a corrective action plan, to public notifications that a service is in remediation, to revocation of FedRAMP Certification; additional administrative actions may be employed as needed.
- Corrective action: Action taken by a cloud service, typically in advance of or in response to administrative action, to make appropriate changes that bring the cloud service into compliance with FedRAMP Certification requirements.
- Corrective Action Plan: A formal document that explains the cloud service’s plan and timeline for completing a necessary corrective action.
- Fully Migrated or Full Migration: This means all VDR/VER rules are being followed by the cloud service, such that all MUST/MUST NOT rules have been fully implemented and all SHOULD/SHOULD NOT rules have been appropriately addressed.
- Fully Implemented: The requirements in the rule are met as intended in their entirety and documented appropriately.
- Appropriately Addressed: This varies by the force of the rule, as follows:
- MUST/MUST NOT: The rule is Fully Implemented.
- SHOULD/SHOULD NOT: The requirements in the rule are met as intended in their entirety and documented appropriately; OR the reason for not doing so has been clearly documented and the resulting risk to federal customer data has been assessed and documented. Partial implementation may also be documented.
- MAY: The requirements in the rule are met as intended partially or entirely; OR the rule is ignored.
Partially (Implemented/Addressed/Etc.): Some part of the rule is implemented/addressed/etc. but not all of it.

> Critical Disclaimer: All content in this section explains our intent at the time this Notice is released, but is subject to change based on direction outside FedRAMP’s direct control. Any changes from this will be the result of either a critical emergent issue that we were unable to predict and address without changes, or the result of direction from a position of authority above FedRAMP.


Consequences for Failure to Fully Implement the VDR/VER by December 7, 2026
Cloud service providers that have not fully implemented the VDR/VER rulesets but respond appropriately to the VDR/VER Implementation Collection with an included reasonable Corrective Action Plan to complete implementation by February 3, 2027 will not be placed into remediation or included in public notification about the gap. All agency customers, FedRAMP Agency Liaisons, and CISA will be notified of the deficiency via internal government communications.

The included reasonable Corrective Action Plan should be written to provide the appropriate customer experience to agency customers in a difficult situation: you are telling them that they are failing to comply with a Binding Operational Directive if they continue to use your service and you are providing assurance to help them understand how this affects the federal information they’ve placed in your service and the mission they’ve built around it. Each agency will make their own determination, based on their own use case of your service and the related risk, of how to respond to this plan. Agencies may consider it a breach of contract, may terminate use of the service, may require additional compensating controls, or may happily accept the Corrective Action Plan without concerns.

If you do not submit a Corrective Action Plan, OR your Corrective Action Plan targets a complete implementation after February 3, 2026, then your cloud service offering will be placed into the “FedRAMP Certified (In Remediation)” status in the FedRAMP Marketplace and will remain in that status until you have fully addressed both rulesets.
Consequences for Failure to Fully Implement the VDR/VER by February 3, 2027
FedRAMP will review all outstanding Corrective Action Plans and request a progress update on February 3 to be delivered no later than 2pm Eastern Time on February 10. Cloud service providers must notify FedRAMP at this time if they believe they will not be able to fully implement their Corrective Action Plan by March 7, 2027. FedRAMP will meet with those cloud service providers to discuss the situation and approve an updated Corrective Action Plan.

The Corrective Action Plan for any cloud service provider who does not respond to this request for a progress update by the deadline will be considered Denied by FedRAMP at that time, and FedRAMP will notify the provider. FedRAMP will take no other administrative action at this time. Cloud service providers must contact FedRAMP to meet about the situation and negotiate for approval of an updated Corrective Action Plan.

FedRAMP will require any Corrective Action Plan extending past March 7, 2027, to include agreement to a red team assessment performed by FedRAMP or a representative of FedRAMP; such providers that do not agree to a red team assessment will have their Corrective Action Plan denied.
Consequences for Failure to Fully Implement the VDR/VER by March 7, 2027
The grace period for VDR/VER implementation ends on March 7, 2027. FedRAMP will immediately be taking administrative action against cloud services who have not met these requirements, as follows:

Cloud services that are not operating under a Corrective Action Plan approved by FedRAMP will have their FedRAMP Certification revoked and all agency customers, Agency Liaisons, CISA, and the public will be notified.
Cloud services that are operating under a Corrective Action Plan approved by FedRAMP will be placed into the “FedRAMP Certified (In Remediation)” status in the Marketplace, and all agency customers, Agency Liaisons, CISA, and the public will be notified. This will count as an adverse administrative action and will last for no less than 30 days, even if the deficiency is otherwise resolved. FedRAMP will initiate red team assessments of these cloud service providers.
Cloud services operating under a Corrective Action Plan who do not cooperate with the previously agreed-upon red team assessment will have their FedRAMP Certification revoked and all agency customers, Agency Liaisons, CISA, and the public will be notified.
Cloud services who fail to show timely progress against their Corrective Action Plan or whose red team assessment shows significant concerns will have their FedRAMP Certification revoked and all agency customers, Agency Liaisons, CISA, and the public will be notified.

All cloud services whose FedRAMP Certification is revoked under these circumstances will be banned from FedRAMP for one year.
Notifying FedRAMP of Implementation after December 7, 2026
Cloud service providers can confirm with FedRAMP when they have implemented the VDR/VER after the Emergency Test by following the instructions on the VDR/VER Implementation Collection Requirements page sent in the Emergency Test. That website will be updated with an alternative URL for a form submission but the requirements and expectations will generally otherwise be the same.


Applicability for Services Pending Certification
Cloud service providers applying for a new FedRAMP Rev5 Certification must meet all applicable requirements to receive that FedRAMP Certification. Any cloud service provider applying for a Rev5 Certification after December 7, 2026, must fully address the VDR/VER to receive their FedRAMP Certification. There is no grace period for new FedRAMP Certifications.

If a cloud service provider applies for a FedRAMP Rev5 Certification after December 7, 2026, they may submit a full Security Decision Record in compliance with the Security Decision Record ruleset. If they have not implemented the Security Decision Record ruleset, then they must supply the same VDR/VER Implementation Information identified at https://preview.fedramp.gov/vdr-implementation.
